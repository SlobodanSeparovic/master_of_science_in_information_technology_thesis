# The Influence of Virtual Reality Environments on Cognitive Load During N-Back Tasks: Implications for Motor Rehabilitation

MSc Thesis, MSc in Software Design, IT University of Copenhagen, July 2025.

**STADS code:** KISPECI1SE

**Authors:**
- Slobodan Separovic
- Carolina Rodrigues Veloso

**Supervisor:** Paolo Burelli

## Abstract

This study examined how virtual reality environments (VEs) influence cognitive load (CL) and engagement during working memory tasks, with implications for neurorehabilitation design. We compared CL and engagement across nature, urban, and control VEs during 2-back and 3-back tasks in 18 participants. Three EEG-derived indexes, the NASA-TLX questionnaire, and behavioral performance metrics were used.

Results showed that only during the more demanding 3-back task did the VE context significantly affect CL. Both natural and urban VEs reduced frontal theta-based CL compared to control. When CL was measured by frontal theta and parietal alpha power, urban environments showed lower CL than control, while differences between nature and control approached but did not reach significance. The engagement index, which is inversely related to CL, was higher in urban versus control VEs; differences involving nature again trended toward significance, but remained non-significant. Across all three EEG-derived indexes, no statistical differences were found between urban and natural VEs.

Behavioral and subjective measures remained consistent across environments, highlighting the greater sensitivity of neurophysiological metrics over traditional performance indicators. These findings suggest that frontal theta-based CL best detects mental workload differences between environments in our study population. Other indexes did not capture significant results, but trends indicate that with larger samples, these differences might also be supported.

Results support that meaningful visual contexts in VR neurorehabilitation help preserve cognitive resources for motor learning, especially as task demands increase. This study underscores the importance of environmental design in optimizing cognitive-motor rehabilitation and supports the feasibility of real-time EEG-based CL monitoring for adaptive VR interventions. Future research should investigate realistic, ecologically valid VEs and extend findings to clinical populations.
